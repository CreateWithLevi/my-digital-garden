This lesson is not about making one more thing. It is about taking an outcome that already exists, placing it with a person or in a situation that needs it, and designing a minimum experiment for use and feedback.

[[en/ai-collaboration-learning-lab|← Back to “Turn Ideas into Outcomes with AI”]]

## Why Package an Outcome

When an outcome stays inside your notes, it can easily become nothing more than “something I organized.”

With a little packaging, it could become:

- a Digital Garden article;
- a short post for a relevant community;
- a GitHub repository;
- a reusable template;
- a small course or lab;
- a service proposal;
- a product prototype.

Packaging is not decoration. It gives the outcome a chance to be used, understood, shared, and tested.

## Five Questions

When you want to bring an outcome into the real world, ask:

1. **What concrete outcome have I already made?**
2. **Who—or what situation—actually needs it?**
3. **Where could they understand or use it?**
4. **How will it come into contact with real use?**
5. **What feedback will I observe?**

## Choose the Context of Use

An outcome does not need to be public, and there is no fixed platform it should go to next. The destination should follow the person and context it is meant to serve:

| Destination                 | What belongs there                            | Main value                                                |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| A current project or job    | Decisions, specs, processes, prototypes       | Directly changes the next action                          |
| Digital Garden              | Long-term thinking, references, public drafts | Builds linkable knowledge assets                          |
| GitHub                      | Code, templates, skills, tools                | Makes the outcome inspectable and usable                  |
| Portfolio                   | Selected cases and outcomes                   | Supports collaboration, services, or career opportunities |
| A community or conversation | A self-contained idea or question             | Gathers responses and new perspectives                    |
| Internal PKM                | Immature or private outcomes                  | Preserves context and future actions                      |

## A Minimum Use Experiment

You do not need to package it as a complete product immediately.

Begin with a 70% version:

1. Choose one person or situation that genuinely needs the outcome.
2. Make a version they can understand, use, or respond to.
3. Ask one trusted person to use it, or place it into real work.
4. Use the response to decide whether to revise, keep, expand, or stop.

The point is:

> Bring the outcome into the real world instead of leaving it in a permanent state of preparation.

## Next Step

Bring the resulting brief back into the relevant project, article, work, or next action.

Then decide which layer it is currently in:

- **Use it first**: Put it into your own work or project and see whether it genuinely helps.
- **Share it next**: Turn it into a version another person can understand or use.
- **Expand it last**: Consider a repository, template, product, service, or course only after repeated demand and feedback appear.
